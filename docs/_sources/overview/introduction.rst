
Introduction
====================

This guide is meant to quickly orient new technical writers, content strategists, engineers, product managers, and start-up organization who lack experience or an existing technical documentation team. 

There are many good books available on the technical writing and content governance; however, this guide serves as a toolbox full of tools, methods, practices, and ideas for anyone who found themselves either willingly or otherwise expected to write “easy-to-use” documentation. 

I don't try to cover every topic in detail. My general approach is to offer starting places for further exploration and experimentation. Think of using tools in a toolkit to complete specific tasks. Using the wrong tool is sometimes worse than not having any tools. A hammer is useless when trying to ladle soup; likewise, not every concept or idea in this guide will apply of make sense in your situation. Take what you think will work. Ignore the rest.

My goal in writing this guide is to add to the technical writing knowledge domain in some small way and to help other writers to gain new insights or apply old tools in a new way.

Assumptions About You
=============================

I assume you...

- have curious mind. You often are the looking into the nooks and crannies to find the hidden features of any application or device you might use. 
- are a fast learner, and often learn new skills or tools just to try something new.
- have a habit of wondering what would happen if I did such and such. You are essentially, one of those people who ask questions to get answers without other agendas.
- have a training and professional experience outside to formal technical writing, but now you are responsible for writing documentation for end users, engineers, software developers, or a technical audience.
